1The idea

You and your friends are the cast of a story told in three acts: the Beginning, the Middle and the Ending. Every act is a Situation: an empathy test in a dim office, a ghost in the hotel ballroom, a trench run, a council over a ring. Each round you choose how your character tackles it, one card of Fate decides how it goes, and the best moments earn you spotlight.

It plays like two games at once:

  • A shared puzzle. Everyone picks one of the Situation’s three choices for what their character wants to do, then how they go in. A single Fate card is turned for everyone, and each roll reads its own number from it.
  • A mad lib. Situations are story templates with blanks: “Rain hammers ____ (Location). A contact says ____ (Object) changes hands tonight…”. The Element cards on the table fill them in, so every game tells a different story. Say how your character does it.

You work together to get through each Situation, because a lost situation scores nothing. But only one of you can be the star of the story: the player with the most spotlight at the end.

2What’s on the table

The Situation card

The story of the act. Its opening text sets the scene, then offers three choices, one per row of the Board, each a speech bubble in a character’s own words (“I slip through the crowd unnoticed”) pointing at the row it belongs to. The blanks are filled in for you, in green on the line, with a small label underneath (VILLAIN, VILLAIN’S SECRET…) saying which card the words come from.

Left of each choice is the space for that row’s Risk: a tile drawn from the bag each round (see 9). A Risk is a resource, shown as a coloured label (Momentum, Reputation…) with two approaches above it. Going in with the approach under the 🔺 is +1 on that row; the one under the 🔻 is −1.

At the bottom are two Escalations: what goes wrong when someone fails badly.

The Board

The Board is where you play your choices. Each row has:

ColumnWhat goes there
MainThe pawn of the player taking the lead on that choice (one per row), with their initiative number.
HelpThe pawn of the one player backing them up (one helper per row).
ApproachThe Main player’s own approach die, turned to how they go in.
EdgeThe Edge die, when the Main player claims it (see 5). An arrow on each row points from Approach to Edge.
TensionOne tall box for all three rows: Tension dice from Escalations, and the Clear seat at its foot (see 11).
SuccessesA stack of spotlight tokens. Each success takes one; empty a row’s stack and you’ve finished the situation.

The banner across the top shows the Edge die at its left end, and a small table for your player count: the successes each act needs (the size of each row’s stack), the difficulty, the resource points you get back before the Middle and the Ending, and the winner’s Story Points. The current act is highlighted.

The Elements

Four cards fill the blanks in every Situation:

  • Location: where it happens. A new Location is drawn for the Middle and the Ending. Its lines are places to use: Climb, Cover, Pit, Crossing, Obstacle, Hidden entry, Slick, Hazard, Enemies.
  • Ally: someone on your side, more or less. Villain: the one behind it all. Both have a Fear, a Goal, a Secret and a Connection.
  • Object: the thing everybody wants. Its Material, Where it’s from, What it’s for and Its flaw.

Each line has a small label (FEAR, CROSSING…) and an approach. People get their names from the name cards tucked above them; the Location gets a describing word (“Neon-lit Street Market”).

Fate

The Fate deck is the game’s dice. Each card gives a number from −2 to +2 to every approach; across the deck each approach gets each number equally often. Only one card is turned per round, and every roll reads the number for its own approach. When the deck runs out, shuffle the discards back in.

The Relationship wheel and Story Points

Your pawn starts each round on the wheel, on the disc in your colour: that’s where it goes back after a roll. Between every two neighbours lies a Relationship card (“Partners on the force…”) showing two approaches. With two players, there’s a single Relationship card in the middle. Relationships are how you help (see 6).

Each player has a numbered initiative token: the order you play in. In the centre is the pile of Story Points: each one is a +1 you can spend on a roll after seeing the Fate card, or the price of swapping a Risk.

The Storyteller token

Each round one player is the Storyteller, shown by the Storyteller token. The token passes clockwise every round. The Storyteller still plays as usual; they also draw the round’s Risks and make compels (see 9).

Card sets

Five card sets tell five kinds of story: Blade Runner, Ghostbusters, Star Wars, Lord of the Rings and Marvel. Each has its own Concepts, Troubles, Assets, Liabilities, Relationships, Elements and Situations; the Fate deck, approaches and resources are shared. Every card shows its set’s name, small, at the bottom of both sides, and the portraits on the Relationship cards come in each set’s style.

Play one set, or tick several for a mash-up: a new deal then takes an even mix from every set (with two sets and four players, two Concepts and two Troubles from each, the Elements two and two), and each act’s Situation and new Location come from the sets used least so far.

Player reference cards

Under each character are two reference cards, one each side up: Each round (the steps of a round) and Your roll (what adds up and how a roll lands).

3Your character

Your character is a grey player board with cards around it:

  • ✔️ Concept (“A Burned-Out Blade Runner…”), on the board’s left place: what you are. It shows one approach (+1 when you go in with it) and one resource (starts 1 higher). Your first name is on the card tucked under it.
  • ✖️ Trouble (“…who drinks to forget”), on the right place: what gets you in trouble. One approach and one resource (starts 1 lower). It only hurts when it’s compelled (see 9). Your last name is tucked under it.
  • ➕ Assets, down the left side (Asset 1 left of the Concept, Asset 2 below it): an item or advantage, +1 on its approach. You hold up to two.
  • ➖ Liabilities, down the right side (Liability 1 right of the Trouble, Liability 2 below it): something that went wrong. They can be compelled. You hold up to two (see 10).
  • Portrait: a Relationship card turned picture-side up, tucked behind the board above your names. Your approach die sits on its corner until you play as Main.

The board holds your six resources, each a die showing its value. They start at 3 (4 for your Concept’s resource, 2 for your Trouble’s) and can go up to 6. You spend them to succeed at a cost (see 7). Above each die are the approaches that guard it (🔺) and squander it (🔻).

Above your area: your badge and your This scene stack (spotlight taken this act, not yet scored), your Story Points, and your Scored spotlight. Everyone starts the game with 1 Story Point.

4Setting up

  1. Choose one or more card sets. With several, deal an even mix (see 2).
  2. Deal each player a Concept and a Trouble, each with a name card tucked under it, an Asset, a portrait, and two reference cards. Set every resource die to 3, then +1 for your Concept’s resource and −1 for your Trouble’s.
  3. Give each player a pawn (on their home disc on the wheel), an approach die, an initiative token and 1 Story Point. Shuffle the initiative numbers. The first player takes the Storyteller token.
  4. Lay out the Relationship cards between neighbours on the wheel, and the Story Point pile in its centre.
  5. Deal the four Elements: Location, Ally, Villain and Object.
  6. Shuffle the Fate deck. Keep the Asset and Liability decks shuffled nearby, and the six Risk tiles (one per resource) in their bag.

5Playing an act

At the start of each act:

  1. Draw a Situation for this act and read it aloud: its blanks are filled in from the Element cards.
  2. Draw a new Location (for the Middle and the Ending). The story moves somewhere new.
  3. Fill the rows: put a stack of spotlight tokens in each row’s Successes box, as many as the act needs.
  4. Set the Edge die. Every Situation is built around one Element line named in it, like “the hidden entry” or “the Villain’s secret”. Turn the Edge die to that line’s approach and put it at the left of the banner. Each round, the first Main player to go in with that approach takes it into their Edge box: +1 on their roll. It goes back at the end of the round.

Then play rounds until the situation is won (someone empties a row’s stack) or lost.

6A round, step by step

  1. Draw the Risks. The Storyteller draws three of the six Risk tiles from the bag and lays one face down on each row, beside its choice. Nobody may look.
  2. Hold your place. If your roll as Main succeeded last round, you may choose to stay Main on the same row: put your pawn straight back in its Main box, without choosing a move. Its Risk is drawn fresh, face down, like the others. Or let it go and choose a move as usual.
  3. Choose your move. In initiative order, everyone else does one of these, choosing a row for what your character wants to do:
    • Main on a free row: put your pawn (with your initiative token) in Main. How you go in comes later.
    • Help a row that already has a Main and no helper yet (one helper per row). It counts if the Main goes in with an approach on one of your two neighbouring Relationship cards (see below).
    • Hold the line in the Clear seat (see 11).
    • Pass, or patch yourself up: give up your move to discard a Liability.
    Before choosing, you may also spend 2 Story Points to discard a Liability.
  4. Turn the Risks over. Each row now shows what it puts on the line.
  5. Swap Risks. In reverse initiative order, each Main player (holding their place or not) may pay 1 Story Point to the pile to swap their row’s Risk with any other row’s (with or without a Main). One swap each; a Risk can change hands more than once.
  6. Go in. In initiative order, each Main player chooses their approach, turns their approach die to it and puts it in the row’s Approach box. A helper whose Relationships show that approach adds +1 (and shares the spotlight if it works); one whose don’t stays on the row but adds nothing and takes nothing.
  7. Compels. A Main player may compel their own Trouble or Liability, and the Storyteller may compel one (see 9).
  8. Turn the Fate card. One card for everyone.
  9. Resolve each Main, in initiative order. One at a time:
    1. Say whose roll it is and how they go in.
    2. The cards that count come out under the Board: the ones that help (Concept, Assets, the helper’s Relationship) on the left, a compelled Trouble or Liability on the right, with any Story Points resting on it. Add up every bonus and penalty.
    3. The Main player decides whether to spend Story Points or resource points.
    4. See how it lands (see 8).
    5. Take any spotlight earned, put the cards back, and return the pawns to their discs on the wheel.
  10. Clear seat. If someone holds it, they remove one Tension die now.
  11. Did it end? If anyone emptied a row this round, the situation is won (see 12). If it was lost, it ends unscored.
  12. End of the round. Tension dice that went in this round start counting now. The Edge die goes back to the banner, the Risk tiles go back in the bag, and the Storyteller token passes clockwise. Then set the new initiative: whoever has the least spotlight on the table (Scored plus This scene) goes first. Ties go to whoever has fewer Story Points.
Why that order?Whoever is behind picks first, and rolls first: they get the first choice of rows and the first chance to finish the situation. Swaps go the other way round, so whoever picked last gets the first chance to trade.
Why face down?So you choose a row for what it says, not for its symbols. Once the Risks are known, the real choice is how you go in: your best approach, or the one the Risk favours.

7Rolling

A roll is the sum of:

✔️ Concept shows your approach+1
➕ Each Asset showing your approach+1 each
The Edge die, if you claimed it+1
Your approach is the row’s Risk 🔺 / 🔻+1 / −1
The helper, if one of their Relationships shows your approach+1
Each Tension die showing your approach (not counting dice that went in this round)−1 each
A compelled Trouble or Liability−1
The Fate card’s number for your approach−2 to +2

Compare it with the act’s difficulty. The difference is your margin.

After the Fate card: spending

Once you see the roll, the Main player may push it up, +1 for each point spent:

  • Story Points: up to 2 per roll.
  • Succeed at a cost: lower the die of the row’s Risk resource by up to 2 points. A die spent down to 0 leaves your sheet: that resource is used up until you get points back.

The spend picker shows where the roll will land as you add points.

Only banked Story PointsYou can only spend Story Points you already have. Story Points earned during this roll (from a compel) wait on the compelled card, and are yours once the roll is over.

8Four ways a roll can land

MarginResultWhat happens
−1 or worseEpic FailureThe Main player draws a Liability, and the situation escalates: they choose one of the Situation’s two Escalations, narrate it, and its Tension die goes in the Tension box.
0 (a tie)FailureNo progress, but nothing lost.
+1SuccessThe Main player takes 1 spotlight token from the row’s stack into This scene, and may hold their place on the row next round. The helper takes 1 too, if their Relationship matched.
+2 or moreEpic SuccessAs a Success, and the Main player draws an Asset.

Nobody loses resources on a failure: resources only go down when you spend them to succeed at a cost. Only the Main player draws a Liability; helping is safe.

9The Storyteller: risks and compels

The Risks

At the start of every round the Storyteller draws three of the six Risk tiles from the bag, one per resource, and lays them face down on the rows. They’re turned over once everyone has chosen a move, and each Main player may then pay 1 Story Point to swap theirs for another row’s (see 6). Nobody chooses who gets which Risk, so nobody can stack the deck: you deal with what you get, by how you go in.

Compels

Your ✖️ Trouble and ➖ Liabilities never count on their own. They can be compelled when a Main player goes in with an approach shown on one of them:

  • You may compel your own, before the Storyteller does: say how it gets in the way.
  • Otherwise the Storyteller may compel one, once per round, and says how it gets in the way.

A compelled card is −1 on the roll, and its owner takes 1 Story Point from the pile (it rests on the card, theirs once the roll is over). When the Storyteller compels you, you may refuse instead by paying 1 Story Point to the pile. Compels happen after the approaches and before the Fate card, so it’s always a bit of a gamble: compel yourself when the −1 can’t change your result.

Where Story Points come fromOne at the start of the game, accepting compels, holding the Clear seat, a third Asset you turn down, and winning a situation. They’re scarce, so spend them on the roll (or the swap) that matters.

10Assets and Liabilities

  • ➕ Assets. Earned on an Epic Success (draw one from the deck). Each gives +1 on its approach. You hold at most two. If you draw a third, either replace one of yours with it, or leave it and take 1 Story Point instead.
  • ➖ Liabilities. Drawn on an Epic Failure: something bad happens to you (a Broken Leg, a Badge Suspended, Slimed); tell the table how. Each shows one approach and can be compelled. You hold at most two. If you’d take a third, you don’t: instead lose one of your Assets, or lower one of your resources by 1, and say how it happens.
  • Getting rid of a Liability: spend 2 Story Points on your turn, or give up your move to patch yourself up. If you already carry a Liability when you fail epically, you may discard an Asset instead of drawing another.

Assets and Liabilities stay from act to act.

11Tension dice and the Clear seat

Every Escalation puts a Tension die in the Tension box: −1 to every roll using that approach, on any row. A new die only starts counting from the next round, so every roll in a round faces the same box.

The box holds two dice. With both out, the next Epic Failure loses the situation: everyone’s This scene tokens go back to the pool, unscored.

Holding the line

  • Instead of playing a row, one player may put their pawn in the Clear seat at the foot of the Tension box.
  • At the end of the round they remove one Tension die, take 1 Story Point, and go first next round.
  • While they sit there, one failure can’t lose the situation.
TipHolding the line costs you a chance to score, but a lost situation costs everyone their tokens. When both dice are out, someone should probably sit in the Clear seat.

12Winning a situation

When a row’s stack is emptied, that row has finished the situation. The round carries on as usual: rows still to roll still roll (and can still fail), and anyone else who empties a row this round finishes it too. At the end of the round the situation is won:

  1. Everyone’s This scene tokens score: move them to Scored spotlight.
  2. The winner is the first Main player to finish. Stamp the Situation with their name, and they take the winner’s Story Points (1 / 2 / 4 for the Beginning / Middle / Ending).
  3. The Underdog Bonus: if the winner wasn’t leading in Scored spotlight going into this act’s scoring, their Story Points are doubled.

Anyone else who finished keeps the spotlight they took, but gets no bonus.

13Between acts and the end of the game

Catching your breath

Before the Middle and before the Ending, everyone gets back 2 resource points (1 with two players), put on any resources you like, up to 6 each. A die you’d used up comes back to your sheet. The Board’s banner shows this as Resources back first.

The end

After the Ending, cash in your leftover Story Points: every 2 Story Points buy 1 spotlight. Then whoever has the most Scored spotlight is the star of the story. Tiebreaker: whoever won the Ending, if they’re one of the tied players.

Afterwards, flip back through the acts (the Act buttons beside the Situation card) and re-tell the story you made.

14A round in play

From a three-player Blade Runner game. It’s the Middle, Enhance: difficulty 3, and each row’s stack holds 2 tokens. Rhea (W) leads with 4 scored spotlight; Dax (X) and Juno (Y) have 2 each. Juno is the Storyteller this round. The Edge die shows 🧠 Wise.

The Risks

Juno draws three Risk tiles from the bag and lays one face down beside each speech bubble: “I zoom in on every corner until I find the clue”, “I get there before ____ (Villain) does” and “I keep the find off the police records”.

The moves

Dax has the least spotlight on the table, so he goes first. He wants to get there first, and takes that row as Main. Juno helps him: her Relationships show ⚡ Swift and 📢 Loud. Rhea takes the clue.

The reveal and a swap

The Risks turn over: the clue risks Reputation (🔺📢 🔻🕶️), getting there risks Control (🔺🐢 🔻💪), the police records Momentum (🔺⚡ 🔻🐢).

Swaps go in reverse initiative order. Rhea is happy with hers. Dax is Swift by nature, and so is Juno’s help, so he pays 1 Story Point to swap his Control for the empty row’s Momentum.

Going in, and a compel

Dax goes in ⚡ Swift: his Concept, the Risk’s 🔺, and Juno’s Relationship all count. Rhea goes in 📢 Loud for her row’s 🔺.

Dax’s Trouble, “…who drinks to forget”, shows ⚡. Juno compels it: he shows up to the chase with a hangover. Dax accepts: −1 on his roll, and a Story Point that rests on the card until the roll is over.

The Fate card

💪 0 · 🧠 −1 · 🐢 +2 · 📢 +1 · ⚡ +1 · 🕶️ −2

Dax (⚡):

✔️ Concept +1 · 🔺 Momentum +1 · Help +1 · ✖️ Trouble (compelled) −1 · Fate +1 = 3 vs 3 → 0: Failure

Dax has no Story Points left after the swap, so he spends 1 point of Momentum (3 → 2): +1, a Success. He takes a token from his row’s stack, and so does Juno, since her Relationship matched.

Rhea (📢):

🔺 Reputation +1 · Fate +1 = 2 vs 3 → −1: Epic Failure

She spends her one Story Point: 0, a Failure. No progress, but nothing lost.

The end of the round

Nobody emptied a row, so the situation goes on. The Risk tiles go back in the bag, Dax takes his Story Point from the Trouble card, and the Storyteller token passes to Rhea. Dax succeeded, so next round he may hold his place on his row, whatever its new Risk turns out to be.

15Quick reference

Each act

  1. New Situation (and a new Location for the Middle and Ending). Read it aloud, filling in the blanks.
  2. Fill each row’s stack with spotlight tokens. Set the Edge die.

Each round

  1. Risks: the Storyteller draws three from the bag, face down, one per row.
  2. Hold your place: if you succeeded as Main last round, you may stay Main on that row (no move needed).
  3. Moves for everyone else, in initiative order, choosing rows for what they say: Main (pawn only) · Help (one per row) · Clear seat · Pass / patch up. 2 Story Points discard a Liability.
  4. Turn the Risks over. Reverse initiative order: each Main may pay 1 Story Point to swap Risks with another row.
  5. Go in in initiative order: each Main sets their approach die. The helper adds +1 only if one of their Relationships shows it.
  6. Compels: a Trouble or Liability showing the Main’s approach. Compel your own, or the Storyteller compels one: −1, and +1 Story Point after the roll (refuse the Storyteller’s for 1 Story Point).
  7. Turn one Fate card.
  8. Resolve each Main in initiative order. After the card: up to 2 Story Points and up to 2 points of the row’s Risk resource, +1 each.
    • −1 or worse, Epic Failure: draw a Liability; choose an Escalation; its Tension die counts from next round.
    • 0, Failure: nothing happens.
    • +1, Success: take a token from the row (and you may hold your place next round); a matching helper takes one too.
    • +2 or more, Epic Success: a token, and draw an Asset.
  9. Clear seat: remove a Tension die, +1 Story Point, go first next round.
  10. Won? A row emptied this round: everyone’s This scene scores; the first to finish wins (stamp, Story Points 1/2/4, doubled for an underdog). Lost? An Epic Failure with 2 Tension dice out (unless someone holds the line).
  11. End of round: new Tension dice start counting; Edge die back; Risks back in the bag; Storyteller passes clockwise; least spotlight goes first.

Remember

  • Bonuses: Concept +1, each Asset +1, Edge die +1, Risk 🔺 +1 / 🔻 −1, a matching helper +1.
  • Up to two Assets (a third: replace one, or take 1 Story Point) and two Liabilities (a third: lose an Asset, or lower a resource by 1). Resources start at 3 and go up to 6.
  • Everyone starts the game with 1 Story Point.
  • Before the Middle and the Ending: 2 resource points back (1 with two players), anywhere.
  • After the Ending, every 2 leftover Story Points buy 1 spotlight. Most Scored spotlight is the star; tiebreaker: whoever won the Ending.
