1The idea

You and your friends are the cast of a story told in three acts: the Beginning, the Middle and the Ending. Every act is a Situation: a body in the rain, a shakedown at the office, a last train out. Each round you choose how your character tackles it, one card of Fate decides how it goes, and the best moments earn you spotlight.

It plays like two games at once:

  • A shared puzzle. Everyone picks one of the Situation’s three choices and an approach to go in with. A single Fate card is turned for everyone, and each roll reads its own number from it.
  • A mad lib. Situations are story templates with blanks: “____ (Villain) is getting out of town tonight with ____ (Object)…”. The Element cards on the table fill them in, so every game tells a different story. Say how your character does it.

You work together to get through each Situation, because a lost situation scores nothing. But only one of you can be the star of the story: the player with the most spotlight at the end.

2What’s on the table

The Situation card

The story of the act. Its opening text sets the scene, then offers three choices, one per row of the Board. The blanks (“____” with a small label under it, like VILLAIN or VILLAIN’S SECRET) are filled in from the Element cards when you read it aloud.

Each choice has a Risk: the resource it puts on the line, shown as a coloured label (Momentum, Reputation…) with two approaches above it. Going in with the approach under the 🔺 is +1 on that row; the one under the 🔻 is −1.

At the bottom are two Escalations: what goes wrong when someone fails badly.

The Board

The Board is where you play your choices. Each row has:

ColumnWhat goes there
MainThe pawn of the player taking the lead on that choice (one per row), with their initiative number.
HelpPawns of players backing them up.
ApproachThe Main player’s own approach die, turned to how they go in.
EdgeThe Edge die, when the Main player claims it (see 5). An arrow on each row points from Approach to Edge.
TensionOne tall box for all three rows: Tension dice from Escalations, and the Clear seat at its foot (see 11).
ObstacleThe Storyteller’s Obstacle die, on one row (see 9).
SuccessesA stack of spotlight tokens. Each success takes one; empty a row’s stack and you’ve finished the situation.

The banner across the top shows the Edge die at its left end, and a small table for your player count: the successes each act needs (the size of each row’s stack), the difficulty, and the winner’s Story Points. The current act is highlighted.

The Elements

Four cards fill the blanks in every Situation:

  • Location: where it happens. A new Location is drawn for the Middle and the Ending. Its lines are places to use: Climb, Cover, Pit, Crossing, Obstacle, Hidden entry, Slick, Hazard, Enemies.
  • Ally: someone on your side, more or less. Villain: the one behind it all. Both have a Fear, a Goal, a Secret and a Connection.
  • Object: the thing everybody wants. Its Material, Where it’s from, What it’s for and Its flaw.

Each line has a small label (FEAR, CROSSING…) and an approach. People get their names from the name cards tucked above them; the Location gets a describing word (“Rain-slicked Hotel”).

Fate

The Fate deck is the game’s dice. Each card gives a number from −2 to +2 to every approach; across the deck each approach gets each number equally often. Only one card is turned per round, and every roll reads the number for its own approach. When the deck runs out, shuffle the discards back in.

The Relationship wheel and Story Points

Your pawn starts each round on the wheel. Between every two neighbours lies a Relationship card (“Childhood runaways…”) showing two approaches. You may help a row if one of the Relationship cards next to you shows that row’s approach. With two players, there’s a single Relationship card in the middle.

Each player has a numbered initiative token: the order you play in. In the centre is the pile of Story Points: each one is a +1 you can spend on a roll after seeing the Fate card.

The Storyteller token

Each round one player is the Storyteller, shown by the Storyteller token with the Obstacle die on it. The token passes clockwise every round. The Storyteller still plays as usual; they also place the Obstacle and make compels (see 9).

3Your character

Your character is a grey player board with cards around it:

  • ✔️ Concept (“A Hard-boiled Detective…”), on the board’s left place: what you are. It shows one approach (+1 when you go in with it) and one resource (starts 1 higher). Your first name is on the card tucked under it.
  • ✖️ Trouble (“…with a drinking problem”), on the right place: what gets you in trouble. One approach and one resource (starts 1 lower). It only hurts when the Storyteller compels it (see 9). Your last name is tucked under it.
  • ➕ Assets, down the left side (Asset 1 left of the Concept, Asset 2 below it): an item or advantage, +1 on its approach. You hold up to two.
  • ➖ Liabilities, down the right side (Liability 1 right of the Trouble, Liability 2 below it): something that went wrong. They can be compelled. You hold up to two.
  • Portrait: a Relationship card turned picture-side up, tucked behind the board above your names. Your approach die sits on its corner until you play as Main.

The board holds your six resources, each a die showing its value. They start at 3 (4 for your Concept’s resource, 2 for your Trouble’s) and can go up to 6. You spend them to succeed at a cost (see 7). Above each die are the approaches that guard it (🔺) and squander it (🔻).

Above your area: your badge and your This scene stack (spotlight taken this act, not yet scored), your Story Points, and your Scored spotlight.

4Setting up

  1. Choose a theme: Film Noir or Space Opera. Each has its own cards; the Fate deck, approaches and resources are shared.
  2. Deal each player a Concept and a Trouble, each with a name card tucked under it, an Asset, and a portrait. Set every resource die to 3, then +1 for your Concept’s resource and −1 for your Trouble’s.
  3. Give each player a pawn, an approach die and an initiative token. Shuffle the initiative numbers. The first player takes the Storyteller token and the Obstacle die.
  4. Lay out the Relationship cards between neighbours on the wheel, and the Story Point pile in its centre.
  5. Deal the four Elements: Location, Ally, Villain and Object.
  6. Shuffle the Fate deck. Keep the Asset and Liability decks shuffled nearby.

5Playing an act

At the start of each act:

  1. Draw a Situation for this act and read it aloud, filling in the blanks from the Element cards.
  2. Draw a new Location (for the Middle and the Ending). The story moves somewhere new.
  3. Fill the rows: put a stack of spotlight tokens in each row’s Successes box, as many as the act needs.
  4. Set the Edge die. Every Situation is built around one Element line named in it, like “the cargo hull hatch” or “the Villain’s secret”. Turn the Edge die to that line’s approach and put it at the left of the banner. Each round, the first Main player to go in with that approach takes it into their Edge box: +1 on their roll. It goes back at the end of the round.

Then play rounds until the situation is won (someone empties a row’s stack) or lost.

6A round, step by step

  1. The Obstacle. Before anyone plays, the Storyteller sets the Obstacle die to an approach, puts it in one row’s Obstacle box, and says how the other side saw it coming (see 9).
  2. Choose your move. In initiative order, each player does one of these:
    • Main on a free row: choose your approach, turn your approach die to it and put it in the row’s Approach box, and put your pawn (with your initiative token) in Main.
    • Help a row that already has a Main, if one of your neighbouring Relationship cards shows the Main’s approach: +1 to their roll, and a spotlight token of your own if it succeeds.
    • Hold the line in the Clear seat (see 11).
    • Pass, or patch yourself up: give up your move to discard a Liability.
    Before choosing, you may also spend 2 Story Points to discard a Liability.
  3. Compels. The Storyteller may compel one Main player’s Trouble or Liability (see 9).
  4. Turn the Fate card. One card for everyone.
  5. Resolve each Main, in initiative order. One at a time:
    1. Say whose roll it is and how they go in.
    2. The cards that count (Concept, Assets, a compelled Trouble or Liability, helpers’ Relationships) come out under the Board. Add up every bonus and penalty.
    3. The Main player decides whether to spend Story Points or resource points.
    4. See how it lands (see 8).
    5. Take any spotlight earned, put the cards back, and return the pawns to the wheel.
  6. Clear seat. If someone holds it, they remove one Tension die now.
  7. Did it end? If anyone emptied a row this round, the situation is won (see 12). If it was lost, it ends unscored.
  8. End of the round. Tension dice that went in this round start counting now. The Edge die goes back to the banner, and the Storyteller token passes clockwise. Then set the new initiative: whoever has the least spotlight on the table (Scored plus This scene) goes first. Ties go to whoever has fewer Story Points.
Why that order?Whoever is behind picks first, and rolls first: they get the first choice of rows and the first chance to finish the situation.

7Rolling

A roll is the sum of:

✔️ Concept shows your approach+1
➕ Each Asset showing your approach+1 each
The Edge die, if you claimed it+1
Your approach is the row’s 🔺 / 🔻+1 / −1
Each helper+1 each
Each Tension die showing your approach (not counting dice that went in this round)−1 each
A compelled Trouble or Liability−1
The Fate card’s number for your approach−2 to +2

Compare it with the act’s difficulty. The difference is your margin.

If the Obstacle is on your row and set to your approach, every bonus above for that approach (Concept, Assets, Edge die and 🔺) is cancelled: cross them out. Helpers and penalties still count.

After the Fate card: spending

Once you see the roll, the Main player may push it up, +1 for each point spent:

  • Story Points: up to 2 per roll.
  • Succeed at a cost: lower the die of the row’s Risk resource by up to 2 points. A die spent down to 0 leaves your sheet: that resource is used up until you get points back.

The spend picker shows where the roll will land as you add points.

Only banked Story PointsYou can only spend Story Points you already have. Story Points earned during this roll (from a compel) are yours once the roll is over.

8Four ways a roll can land

MarginResultWhat happens
−2 or worseEpic FailureThe Main player draws a Liability, and the situation escalates: they choose one of the Situation’s two Escalations, narrate it, and its Tension die goes in the Tension box.
−1 or 0FailureNo progress, but nothing lost.
+1SuccessThe Main player takes 1 spotlight token from the row’s stack into This scene. Each helper takes 1 too.
+2 or moreEpic SuccessAs a Success, and the Main player draws an Asset.

Nobody loses resources on a failure: resources only go down when you spend them to succeed at a cost. Only the Main player draws a Liability; helping is safe.

9The Storyteller: Obstacles and compels

The Obstacle

Before anyone plays, the Storyteller puts the Obstacle die on one row, set to one approach: the other side was ready for that. Going into that row with that approach cancels all its bonuses this round. It never pushes a roll below what it would be with no bonuses, so it hurts most the player leaning hardest on that approach.

Compels

Your ✖️ Trouble and ➖ Liabilities never count on their own, and you can’t bring them up yourself. Once per round, after the moves and before the Fate card, the Storyteller may compel one of them when a Main player:

  • goes in with their Trouble’s or a Liability’s approach, or
  • plays a row that risks their Trouble’s resource.

The Storyteller says how it gets in the way. The Main player either accepts (−1 on the roll, and 1 Story Point from the pile, theirs once the roll is over) or refuses by paying 1 Story Point to the pile.

Where Story Points come fromAccepting compels, holding the Clear seat, and winning a situation. That’s all: they’re scarce, so spend them on the roll that matters.

10Assets and Liabilities

  • ➕ Assets. Earned on an Epic Success (draw one from the deck). Each gives +1 on its approach. You hold at most two; if you’d have a third, keep the two you prefer.
  • ➖ Liabilities. Drawn on an Epic Failure: something bad happens to you (a Broken Leg, Out of Ammo, Shaky Hands); tell the table how. Each shows one approach and can be compelled. You hold at most two.
  • Getting rid of a Liability: spend 2 Story Points on your turn, or give up your move to patch yourself up. If you already carry a Liability when you fail epically, you may discard an Asset instead of drawing another.

Assets and Liabilities stay from act to act.

11Tension dice and the Clear seat

Every Escalation puts a Tension die in the Tension box: −1 to every roll using that approach, on any row. A new die only starts counting from the next round, so every roll in a round faces the same box.

The box holds two dice. With both out, the next Epic Failure loses the situation: everyone’s This scene tokens go back to the pool, unscored.

Holding the line

  • Instead of playing a row, one player may put their pawn in the Clear seat at the foot of the Tension box.
  • At the end of the round they remove one Tension die, take 1 Story Point, and go first next round.
  • While they sit there, one failure can’t lose the situation.
TipHolding the line costs you a chance to score, but a lost situation costs everyone their tokens. When both dice are out, someone should probably sit in the Clear seat.

12Winning a situation

When a row’s stack is emptied, that row has finished the situation. The round carries on as usual: rows still to roll still roll (and can still fail), and anyone else who empties a row this round finishes it too. At the end of the round the situation is won:

  1. Everyone’s This scene tokens score: move them to Scored spotlight.
  2. The winner is the first Main player to finish. Stamp the Situation with their name, and they take the winner’s Story Points (1 / 2 / 4 for the Beginning / Middle / Ending).
  3. The Underdog Bonus: if the winner wasn’t leading in Scored spotlight going into this act’s scoring, their Story Points are doubled.

Anyone else who finished keeps the spotlight they took, but gets no bonus.

13Between acts and the end of the game

Catching your breath

Between acts, everyone gets back 2 resource points (1 with two players), put on any resources you like, up to 6 each. A die you’d used up comes back to your sheet.

The end

After the Ending, cash in your leftover Story Points: every 2 Story Points buy 1 spotlight. Then whoever has the most Scored spotlight is the star of the story. Tiebreaker: whoever won the Ending, if they’re one of the tied players.

Afterwards, flip back through the acts (the Act buttons beside the Situation card) and re-tell the story you made.

14A round in play

From a three-player Film Noir game. It’s the Middle: difficulty 3, and each row’s stack holds 2 tokens. Kitty (W) leads with 4 scored spotlight; Duke (X) and Sam (Y) have 2 each. Sam is the Storyteller this round. The Edge die shows 🧠 Wise.

The Obstacle

Sam puts the Obstacle on row C (“trace where ____ (Villain)’s call came from”), set to 🧠 Wise: “they’ve already wiped the line.” That’s the row’s 🔺 approach, and Kitty’s Concept is Wise too.

The moves

Duke has the least spotlight on the table, so he goes first. He takes row A, “make ____ (Villain) believe you already have ____ (Object)”, going in 📢 Loud: that’s the row’s 🔺 (it risks Reputation) and his Concept’s approach. Sam helps him: a Relationship card between them shows 📢.

Kitty sees the Obstacle on C and takes row B instead, going in 🕶️ Subtle, the 🔺 for its Information risk.

The compel

Duke’s Trouble, “…with misplaced loyalty”, shows 📢. Sam compels it: Duke can’t lie to save the man who once saved him. Duke accepts: −1 on his roll, and a Story Point once it’s over.

The Fate card

💪 0 · 🧠 −1 · 🐢 +2 · 📢 +2 · ⚡ −2 · 🕶️ +1

Duke, row A (📢):

✔️ Concept +1 · 🔺 Reputation +1 · Help +1 · ✖️ Trouble (compelled) −1 · Fate +2 = 4 vs 3 → +1: Success

Duke spends his one Story Point: +2, an Epic Success. He takes a token from row A’s stack, Sam takes the other, and Duke draws an Asset. Row A is empty: the situation is finished.

Kitty, row B (🕶️):

🔺 Information +1 · Fate +1 = 2 vs 3 → −1: Failure

The situation is already finished, but Kitty still rolls as usual. She spends 2 points of Information (3 → 1): +1, a Success. She takes a token from row B.

The end of the round

The situation is won. Everyone’s This scene tokens score: Kitty 5, Duke 3, Sam 3. Duke finished first, so he’s the winner: the Middle is worth 2 Story Points, and since he wasn’t leading, the Underdog Bonus doubles them to 4. Next act, he has plenty to spend.

15Quick reference

Each act

  1. New Situation (and a new Location for the Middle and Ending). Read it aloud, filling in the blanks.
  2. Fill each row’s stack with spotlight tokens. Set the Edge die.

Each round

  1. Obstacle: the Storyteller puts it on a row and an approach.
  2. Moves in initiative order: Main (choose an approach, set your die) · Help (needs a Relationship showing the Main’s approach) · Clear seat · Pass / patch up. 2 Story Points discard a Liability.
  3. Compel (Storyteller, once): Trouble or Liability matches the approach, or the row risks the Trouble’s resource. −1 and +1 Story Point, or refuse for 1 Story Point.
  4. Turn one Fate card.
  5. Resolve each Main in initiative order. After the card: up to 2 Story Points and up to 2 points of the row’s resource, +1 each.
    • −2 or worse, Epic Failure: draw a Liability; choose an Escalation; its Tension die counts from next round.
    • −1 or 0, Failure: nothing happens.
    • +1, Success: take a token from the row; each helper takes one too.
    • +2 or more, Epic Success: a token, and draw an Asset.
  6. Clear seat: remove a Tension die, +1 Story Point, go first next round.
  7. Won? A row emptied this round: everyone’s This scene scores; the first to finish wins (stamp, Story Points 1/2/4, doubled for an underdog). Lost? An Epic Failure with 2 Tension dice out (unless someone holds the line).
  8. End of round: new Tension dice start counting; Edge die back; Storyteller passes clockwise; least spotlight goes first.

Remember

  • Bonuses: Concept +1, each Asset +1, Edge die +1, row 🔺 +1 / 🔻 −1, each helper +1. The Obstacle cancels them for its approach on its row.
  • Up to two Assets and two Liabilities. Resources start at 3 and go up to 6.
  • Between acts: 2 resource points back (1 with two players), anywhere.
  • After the Ending, every 2 leftover Story Points buy 1 spotlight. Most Scored spotlight is the star; tiebreaker: whoever won the Ending.
